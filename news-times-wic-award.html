TIMES recognized with the 2026 World Internet Conference Leading Science and Technology Award

November 2026 · Wuzhen, China

A research achievement from Professor Cheng Sun’s team at USTC has been recognized for advancing the integration of spatial immunology, computation, and translational cancer research.

A research achievement from Professor Cheng Sun's team at the University of Science and Technology of China (USTC) has been recognized with the 2026 World Internet Conference Leading Science and Technology Award in Wuzhen, China.

The award recognizes the team's development of TIMES (Tumor Immune MicroEnvironment Spatial Scoring System), an approach integrating spatial immune profiling, computational analysis, and experimental immunology to improve our understanding of cancer progression and support precision oncology.

The World Internet Conference Leading Science and Technology Award recognizes scientific and technological innovations with significant potential to advance research, industry, and society. This recognition highlights the growing importance of interdisciplinary approaches that connect biomedical research with emerging computational technologies.

Understanding cancer through spatial immunology

The tumor microenvironment is a complex ecosystem in which malignant cells, immune cells, and stromal components interact within a highly organized tissue architecture. Increasing evidence suggests that the spatial relationships among these cells can influence cancer progression and therapeutic responses.

In research published in Nature in 2025, Professor Sun's team developed TIMES to characterize spatial immune organization and predict postoperative recurrence in hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC).

By integrating spatially resolved molecular information with clinical data, the study identified immune features associated with disease outcomes. Importantly, the researchers complemented computational analyses with experimental investigations of natural killer (NK) cell biology, providing mechanistic insights into how spatial immune organization contributes to tumor control.

These findings demonstrate the potential of combining spatial immunology and computational approaches to generate clinically relevant insights into cancer.

Connecting AI, biological mechanisms, and clinical translation

The recognition at the World Internet Conference reflects a broader scientific direction pursued by the Sun Lab: integrating advanced computational approaches with mechanistic immunology and translational cancer research.

While artificial intelligence offers powerful opportunities to identify patterns in complex biological datasets, understanding the functional significance of these patterns requires rigorous experimental investigation.

The team's research therefore emphasizes a complementary approach in which spatial profiling and computational analysis inform biological hypotheses, functional experiments investigate underlying mechanisms, and clinical studies assess their potential relevance to patient care.

Looking ahead, the laboratory aims to further develop this interdisciplinary framework to advance spatially informed biomarkers, immune therapeutic target discovery, and precision cancer medicine.

Advancing interdisciplinary collaboration

The award also underscores the importance of collaboration across immunology, oncology, pathology, computational biology, and biotechnology.

The development of TIMES has benefited from contributions by researchers, students, clinical collaborators, and multidisciplinary partners.

Building on this foundation, the Sun Lab seeks to strengthen international academic and industry collaborations at the intersection of AI, spatial immunology, functional biology, and cancer therapeutics, with the long-term goal of translating fundamental discoveries into clinically meaningful applications.
