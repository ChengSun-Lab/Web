Review article

Spatially instructed checkpoints in antitumor NK cell immunity

November 2026 · Trends in Immunology

A new review from Prof. Cheng Sun and colleagues examines how the spatial organization of solid tumors shapes NK-cell access, function, and antitumor immunity.

Immune checkpoints are not just molecular. They are spatial.

Why do natural killer (NK) cells, despite their remarkable antitumor potential, often struggle to control solid tumors?

We typically think of immune checkpoints as inhibitory receptors and signaling pathways. But an equally important question is often overlooked: Where does immune suppression occur?

Prof. Cheng Sun is pleased to share a recent review in Trends in Immunology: Spatially instructed checkpoints in antitumor NK cell immunity.

Together with Ruifeng Wang and Peng Sun, the article explores the concept of spatially instructed checkpoints—inhibitory programs shaped by the local tissue environment, whose functional consequences depend on where NK cells are located and which cells they encounter.

Three spatial scales

The review examines this concept across three interconnected spatial scales:

  • Tissue architecture: Can NK cells physically reach malignant cells?
  • Cellular neighborhoods: Do surrounding immune and stromal cells support or suppress NK-cell activity?
  • Cytotoxic synapses: What determines whether direct contact results in effective tumor killing?

These distinctions may have important implications for cancer immunotherapy. A tumor that excludes NK cells may require a fundamentally different therapeutic strategy from one in which NK cells are present but functionally suppressed.

Looking ahead, the integration of spatial immunology, AI-driven tissue analysis, and mechanistic experimentation could help move the field toward more precise, biologically informed immunotherapies.

AI can help us recognize spatial patterns. Experimental immunology is essential to understand their mechanisms and identify actionable interventions.

Prof. Sun is particularly interested in connecting these insights with translational research and welcomes discussions with academic and industry colleagues working at the intersection of spatial biology, AI, and cancer immunotherapy.
